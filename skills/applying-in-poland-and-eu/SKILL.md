# Applying in Poland and the EU

## Overview

Generic CV advice assumes US norms, and Polish CV advice online is dominated by content that
contradicts the Polish regulator. The three decisions that actually differ by market are the
RODO clause, the photo, and the date of birth.

Sources and verbatim legal text: `docs/research/2026-09-03-cv-ats-linkedin-sources.md` §3.1–3.3.

**Legal position verified 2026-09-03; re-verify against UODO annually.** These are statements
about current Polish law, and the cost of repeating a stale one is high. If the check is
overdue, say so rather than asserting the position with unearned confidence.

## The RODO clause is not required

This contradicts most Polish-language advice, including law-firm publications, so state the
basis when the user pushes back.

**Art. 22¹ §1 Kodeksu pracy** is a *closed* catalogue of what an employer demands from a
candidate: name and surname, date of birth, contact details indicated by that person,
education, professional qualifications, employment history. Processing this needs no consent.

**Art. 22¹a §1** makes consent a basis only for data *outside* that catalogue. **§2** is
decisive:

> Brak zgody… lub jej wycofanie, **nie może być podstawą niekorzystnego traktowania** osoby
> ubiegającej się o zatrudnienie… a także **nie może stanowić przyczyny uzasadniającej odmowę
> zatrudnienia**.

**UODO**, the Polish data protection authority, states it directly:

> Do przetwarzania wyżej wymienionych danych **nie jest potrzebna zgoda. Dotychczasowa
> praktyka zamieszczenia w liście motywacyjnym CV zgody na przetwarzanie danych w celach
> rekrutacyjnych nie jest właściwa.**

The GDPR information duty falls on the **employer**, not the candidate.

### The one legitimate use

Consent for **future** recruitment processes is genuinely outside art. 22¹ and genuinely
requires consent. There is no officially prescribed wording — sites presenting "the official
2026 clause" are inventing it. A defensible narrow form:

> Wyrażam zgodę na przetwarzanie moich danych osobowych zawartych w niniejszym CV przez
> [pracodawca] w celu udziału w przyszłych procesach rekrutacyjnych prowadzonych przez ten
> podmiot, przez okres [12 miesięcy] od dnia przesłania aplikacji.

Time-bounded and specific. Never a blanket "I consent to processing for recruitment
purposes" — that is exactly the practice UODO calls inappropriate.

### Decision rule

Omit by default. Include the narrow future-recruitment clause only if the candidate wants to
stay in the employer's database, or if a specific job ad demands a clause — and in that case
say plainly that the demand has no legal basis, since the candidate may still prefer to
comply.

## Photo

**Omit by default in every target market.**

UODO: "Przepisy kodeksu pracy **nie nakładają obowiązku** przekazywania pracodawcy przez
kandydata do pracy swojego zdjęcia." It sits outside the catalogue, so it rests on consent,
and under §2 declining cannot lawfully justify rejection.

In the US the EEOC instructs employers not to request photographs, and many screen out or
redact applications containing one.

It also consumes prime page-one space that belongs to employer names, and it adds no signal
about engineering ability.

**Honest limit:** the country-by-country picture for the EU (photos conventional in Germany,
Austria and Central Europe; avoided in the UK, Ireland, the Netherlands and the Nordics) is
widely-held practitioner knowledge that could not be confirmed from a primary source. The
no-photo default makes the uncertainty inconsequential, so don't assert the pattern with
false confidence.

## Date of birth — a genuine Poland/US conflict

Date of birth is a **statutory field** in the Polish catalogue and a **liability** in the US,
where the ADEA protects candidates from 40 onward and the EEOC treats such data as evidence
of discriminatory intent.

This is not a superset — one CV cannot satisfy both. Treat it as a per-market variable.

**Omit by default.** Art. 22¹ §5 makes disclosure a declaration by the candidate, and §2
protects against adverse treatment, so omitting it is lawful in Poland. Add only if a
specific Polish employer's process requires it. Also avoid graduation years that pin the age
precisely on a US-facing CV.

Marital status, PESEL, parents' names, place of birth and full residential address are all
outside the recruitment catalogue. Legal to include, pointless, and they expand the data
surface for no benefit. City is enough; a full street address on a CV posted online is a
fraud risk.

Nationality is the useful exception: work authorisation is a real operational question, and
"EU citizen — no sponsorship required" is a factual line rather than a protected-characteristic
disclosure. Stating work eligibility is not the same as stating nationality.

## Europass: do not use for tech

Europass is the official EU format and it is genuinely useful for cross-border comparison of
formal qualifications, regulated professions, academic mobility, and applications to public
sector and EU institutions.

For software engineering hiring it is inappropriate: the schema has **no field for GitHub, a
portfolio, or a technology stack** — the primary evidence of an engineer's work. The personal
skills section consumes half a page for content no reader trusts. The logo and the
"Curriculum Vitae" title waste the most valuable space on the document.

Use it only when an employer or process explicitly demands it. It is never the default.

Note that the EU's own Europass guidance tells you to add a photograph, directly contradicting
US career-services guidance. Sources genuinely disagree here; don't paper over it.

## Length

Two A4 pages is the normal working maximum in Europe, and unlike the US "one page per decade"
rule it is framed as a ceiling for everyone rather than a seniority reward. One page is safe
everywhere if the material genuinely fits.

## Language

Match the language of the job posting. A Polish-language posting from a Polish company gets a
Polish CV; an English-language posting, including from Polish employers hiring for
international teams, gets English. When in doubt for Warsaw tech roles, English is the safer
default — but never assume it without checking the posting.

**The language of the conversation and the language of the artifact are separate decisions.**
Talk to the candidate in whatever language they wrote to you in, and produce the CV in the
language the posting demands. Say which language you chose for the document and why.
